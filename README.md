# RideSafety — Designing a Safer Ride-Sharing Experience for Women in Dhaka

**A User-Centered Mobile Application Prototype for Women's Ride-Sharing Safety**

## 📌 Project Overview

RideSafety is a high-fidelity mobile application prototype designed to explore how digital safety features can improve the ride-sharing experience for women in Dhaka, Bangladesh.

The project focuses on making safety controls more accessible, supporting trusted-contact communication, improving awareness of unexpected route changes, and enabling privacy-conscious incident reporting.

The prototype was designed using a user-centered approach informed by survey responses, semi-structured interviews, and thematic analysis. The goal is to explore a more proactive, accessible, and privacy-aware safety experience for women using ride-sharing services.

## 🎯 Problem Statement

Women using ride-sharing and other transportation services may experience safety concerns before, during, and after a journey.

Common concerns include:

- Inappropriate driver behavior and unwanted comments.
- Traveling alone at night or through isolated and unfamiliar roads.
- Unexpected changes to the planned route.
- Difficulty finding emergency safety features quickly.
- Limited access to trusted support during emergencies.
- Privacy concerns when reporting safety incidents.
- Dependence on internet connectivity for digital safety tools.

Existing safety features may be difficult to discover or require several interactions when a user needs help quickly.

RideSafety explores how these challenges can be addressed through a user-centered mobile interface that prioritizes accessibility, trusted support, privacy, and proactive safety awareness.

## 💡 Proposed Solution

RideSafety presents a high-fidelity prototype that brings important safety-related actions into a more accessible mobile experience.

The design emphasizes visible emergency controls, simple user workflows, discreet assistance options, and privacy-conscious community reporting.

### Core Features

#### 1. One-Tap Live Location Sharing
A proposed interface for quickly sharing trip location information with selected trusted contacts.

#### 2. Driver and Vehicle Verification
A vehicle safety-check interface that allows users to review or record driver descriptions, vehicle types, and license plate information before a trip.

#### 3. Route Deviation Alerts
A proposed safety interface that compares the expected route with the current route and presents an alert when an unexpected deviation is detected.

#### 4. Emergency SOS
An emergency interface designed to make SOS actions visible and accessible, with a confirmation countdown and trusted-contact alert concept.

#### 5. Anonymous Incident Reporting
A privacy-focused reporting workflow that allows users to select an incident category, provide a description, attach a photo, and include location information.

#### 6. Discreet SOS Gestures
A configuration interface for alternative SOS activation methods, including shaking the phone and using physical button gestures.

#### 7. Fake Call Feature
A simulated incoming-call interface intended to help users leave uncomfortable situations more comfortably.

#### 8. Evidence Recording
A proposed recording interface with GPS, audio, and video status indicators to illustrate how trip-related evidence might be captured.

#### 9. Community Safety Feed
A community interface concept for viewing nearby safety reports, unsafe locations, safer spots, and vehicle-related concerns.

#### 10. Offline-First Safety Concepts
The prototype explores offline SMS fallback for trusted-contact alerts and cached community reports for situations where internet access is unavailable.

**Note:** These features are represented as prototype designs and proposed interactions. They have not been implemented or verified as operational software.

## 🔬 Research Methodology

The project follows a user-centered research and design approach.

### 1. Survey

A survey was conducted with 16 female ride-sharing users in Dhaka to understand their safety concerns, existing safety practices, feature preferences, and privacy expectations.

### 2. Semi-Structured Interviews

Two in-depth interviews were conducted with female ride-sharing users. The interviews explored daily travel experiences, emergency response preferences, location sharing, connectivity challenges, and incident reporting.

### 3. Affinity Diagramming

Survey responses and interview findings were organized into thematic groups to identify recurring concerns and translate them into design requirements.

### 4. Thematic Analysis

Six key themes were identified:

- Safety Concerns
- Verification
- Trusted Support
- Emergency Alerts
- Awareness Gap
- Privacy and Trust

### 5. Prototype Design

The findings informed the design of a high-fidelity mobile interface focused on accessible safety controls, trip monitoring concepts, emergency assistance, and anonymous reporting.

### 6. Task-Based Usability Evaluation

The prototype was evaluated through seven tasks:

- Account setup
- Adding trusted contacts
- Configuring an SOS gesture
- Performing a vehicle safety check
- Using the fake-call feature
- Submitting an anonymous report
- Viewing the community safety map

## 📊 Research Findings

The survey highlighted several important user needs.

| Safety concern or feature | Finding |
|---|---|
| Driver behavior and comments | Reported by 7 participants |
| Isolated or unfamiliar roads | Reported by 6 participants |
| Unexpected route changes | Reported by 5 participants |
| One-tap live location | Requested by 13 participants |
| SOS to a chosen contact | Requested by 7 participants |
| Verified driver and vehicle | Requested by 6 participants |

These findings informed the prioritization of location sharing, verification, route monitoring, and trusted-contact support in the prototype.

## 🧪 Usability Evaluation Results

The research paper reports the following task completion rates:

| Evaluation task | Success rate |
|---|---:|
| Account setup | 100% |
| Trusted contacts | 100% |
| SOS gesture | 90% |
| Vehicle safety check | 100% |
| Fake call | 100% |

Reported average ratings were:

The paper reports these results as prototype usability findings, not evidence of real-world reductions in harassment or improved emergency response.

## 🌟 Key Design Principles

### Accessibility
Important safety actions should remain visible and easy to find instead of being hidden in complex menus.

### Trusted Support
Users should be able to choose trusted contacts for location sharing and emergency notifications.

### Privacy and Anonymity
Incident reporting should minimize unnecessary exposure of a user's identity.

### Proactive Safety Awareness
Driver and vehicle checks, route monitoring concepts, and timely alerts can help users identify potential concerns earlier.

### Offline Resilience
The design explores SMS fallback and cached community information to address unreliable internet connectivity.

### User Choice
Discreet activation methods and configurable trusted contacts allow users to choose approaches that suit their needs.

## ⚠️ Limitations

The project has several limitations:

- The survey included only 16 participants and was primarily representative of young adult students.
- The research included two in-depth interviews.
- The prototype was evaluated for usability, not real-world safety effectiveness.
- GPS tracking, SOS delivery, SMS fallback, evidence storage, and route detection are design concepts rather than verified working features.
- Battery consumption, false gesture triggers, evidence backup, and community-map usability remain areas for further investigation.

The findings should therefore be interpreted as preliminary design evidence rather than generalized conclusions about all women in Dhaka.
